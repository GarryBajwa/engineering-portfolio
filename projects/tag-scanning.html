Project overview

I independently developed and rolled out a MES-style manufacturing application, beginning with departmental barcode tracking and expanding to plant-wide production records, auto-updating KPIs, employee-work analysis, schedule progress and supervisor decisions. The new information made production and performance patterns visible where no reliable historical comparison previously existed. Around this core, I built or contributed to complementary engineering tools for TPO preparation, CAD/CAM validation and shop-floor execution. I also introduced the software to employees and supervisors, handled exceptions, and refined it from real production feedback.

Case 01 · The manufacturing challenge

Replacing scattered production updates with traceable events

Paper records and verbal handoffs made it difficult to understand where a product was, which work was complete, how long processing took and which department needed support.

Limited real-time visibilityProduction status often depended on manually checking work or asking departments for updates.
Disconnected labour informationEmployee activity, completed tasks and product-processing durations were difficult to analyze consistently.
Exception-heavy workflowsMissed scans, held jobs and production changes required a practical recovery process rather than a perfect-data assumption.
Planning without timely evidenceSupervisors needed actionable information on pending work, capacity, bottlenecks and labour requirements.

Case 02 · Development and rollout

Built for one department, expanded across the plant

The implementation was iterative: solve the immediate scanning problem, make the interface usable in production, and then extend the same traceability principles into other departments and connected tools.

01 — Department pilotIntroduced a practical digital scan workflow for a specific production area, replacing informal status tracking with recorded events.
02 — Additional workflowsExpanded department-specific production records, employee identification, status changes and schedule-level visibility.
03 — Supervisor and management functionsAdded search, exceptions, drill-down, daily activity and reporting features to turn recorded scans into operational information.
04 — Connected manufacturing applicationsUsed the central Flask system as an integration point for custom CNC-side and related desktop software.
Implementation ownership: I developed the software, deployed it to production workstations, trained employees and supervisors, collected feedback and continued refining the workflows. Production workflows were expanded and refined after rollout.

Case 03 · Software architecture

Python services, browser clients and Excel-backed workflows

The system combined a centralized application server with department-specific scanning interfaces and reporting processes suited to the plant's existing tools and data formats.

Central serverPython, Flask and Flask-SocketIO provided the application backbone, API interfaces and live communication. The project's technical stack also included Eventlet and APScheduler.
Scanning interfacesHTML, CSS and JavaScript interfaces accepted keyboard/USB scanner input and supported QuaggaJS Code 128 camera scanning.
Data processingDepartment-specific workbook managers, employee-activity modules, Pandas and OpenPyXL supported operational records, analysis and exports.
External application communicationCustom PC applications used the Flask server's APIs to communicate with the central production platform; Socket.IO supported real-time status and updates.

Case 04 · Production traceability

From barcode events to actionable production status

Employees entered their ID at a station, scanned production tags and created time-based work records. Supervisors could then examine completion progress and resolve exceptions.

Employee-to-work associationStation sign-in using an employee ID associated scanning activity with the person carrying out the task. This description does not imply an independently verified security/permissions model.
Time-stamped task historyStart and end records supported product traceability, work-in-progress status and processing-time calculations across manufacturing operations.
Schedule-to-detail navigationManagement dashboards summarized schedules and completion percentages, with drill-down to the work and status behind a schedule.
Supervisor exception recoveryOperational controls included Hold, Missed, Skipped, Override Passed and Cancel, supporting practical review and resolution of incomplete or unusual scan histories.
The application handled imperfect shop-floor data through operator and supervisor workflows. Specific role-authorization mechanisms are intentionally not claimed without code-level verification.

Measuring product work—not just attendance

Start/end timestamps made it possible to analyze actual time associated with manufacturing products, distinguish recorded processing from gaps between tasks and support more realistic production planning.

Product processing timeRecorded task durations supported actual labour-time estimates for specific products and automatic population of product specification information.
Workday utilizationSumming recorded production time and comparing it with available shift time helped management investigate recurring gaps and opportunities for process improvements.
Average-rate costingThe central system applied a configurable average hourly rate across recorded labour time to estimate product-related labour costs.
Individual analysis and coachingA related performance evaluation application could analyze historical employee activity data—including approximately a year of records—to estimate individual labour rates and inform coaching, cross-training and fairer evaluations with appropriate human context.
Recorded work times and automatically generated KPIs helped supervisors and managers assess production capability at plant, department and individual levels. In areas where consistent metrics had not existed, the new records created the baseline for future comparisons rather than a defensible before-and-after percentage. Task timestamps alone cannot measure quality or prove employee productivity without context for rework, setup, downtime and scanning compliance.

CNC-side softwareA desktop application loaded G-code at the machine, communicated with the Flask platform for production-ticket validation and tag updates, and displayed material-use guidance to the operator.
G-code validationA connected validation tool assessed cutting programs and sheet usage to help determine whether a recorded reusable leftover could be used or a full sheet was needed.
Leftover-material managementA separate application used by design engineering tracked reusable sheet remnants. Suitable leftover material could be associated with future cutting requirements.
Ticketed reuse workflowWhen a full sheet was required, an available remainder could be set aside with a ticket and recorded in the leftover system for later suggestions. The leftover-data store is distinct from the confirmed Excel-backed production records.
TPO schedule generatorA companion engineering utility generated schedule-related TPO outputs instead of requiring repetitive manual preparation. Based on operational experience, this avoided approximately 20–40 minutes of preparation per schedule and reduced opportunities for transcription or selection errors; a measured error-reduction rate is not available.
2D drawing and CNC compatibility checksRelated DXF / CAD-CAM tooling automatically checks 2D drawing compatibility with the selected machine as G-code is generated, before each machine run. The reported check executes in under one second and flags issues early; resolving an incompatible drawing is a separate process. This replaced slow, manual discovery of some problems that previously remained unresolved for extended periods.
Style-name and kit-data governanceA separate controlled CAD/CAM workflow scanned 3,991 kit records and supported exact matching, controlled renaming, conflict checks, dry runs, exception review and decision logs. Uncertain matches were held for review rather than silently changed.
Engineering-to-production feedbackLinking schedule preparation, drawing validation, production tickets and CNC-side status helped expose upstream engineering inconsistencies before they became recurring shop-floor issues. The companion utilities are not assumed to communicate directly with every central-server API.

Case 07 · Data continuity and reporting

Practical storage, reconciliation and export workflows

Rather than requiring a new enterprise database, the production platform used structured Excel records and familiar output formats suited to existing operations and integrations.

Monthly department workbooksProduction work was recorded in department-specific monthly spreadsheets; employee work records were also maintained in daily activity workbooks.
Main and backup recordsThe workflow used primary and backup workbooks, with end-of-day synchronization and next-day selection based on the latest modified record.
Production and payroll exportsData processing supported CSV/text-based production and payroll data export workflows, connecting manufacturing records with existing operational processes.
Historical backtracking and analyticsTime-stamped work history allowed management and engineering to revisit past events, investigate schedule or production discrepancies and analyze performance across daily, weekly, monthly and longer periods. These records also form an input for future staffing, material and infrastructure planning models.
Implementation boundary: This account does not claim transaction-level database locking, zero data loss, an SQL backend or a completed database migration. The exact file-access and concurrency strategy is not publicly documented.

Case 08 · Results and engineering value

A software project with real operational adoption

The greatest result was not only labour time saved: the platform established continuously available, scan-derived evidence of what the plant, its departments and workstations could produce, supported by related engineering automation and a reusable history of operating decisions.

Automatically updated KPIsGenerated up-to-date schedule progress, pending-work indicators and operational summaries from production records, reducing recurring manual status collection and giving leadership a shared plant and department view.
Measurable capability and better decisionsRecorded output and task durations helped identify bottlenecks and practical production capacity at plant, department and employee levels, supporting coaching, cross-training, hiring and workload decisions. Historical data made future material and infrastructure demand analysis possible; automated forecasting is not claimed.
Exception handling and accountabilityRetained search and historical review of scan events, with supervisor handling for held, missed, skipped and overridden statuses. Recoverable exceptions made the system practical without assuming every operator action was perfect.
Engineering and operational leverageConnected scanner clients, centralized software, reporting and CNC-side applications while complementing them with schedule and CAD/CAM automation. This allowed individual process improvements to contribute to a wider traceable manufacturing workflow.
Live KPI distribution to shared screensThe separate Centralized Multi-Screen Display Platform presented Tag-Scanning production metrics on common-area and shop-floor screens. The same Raspberry Pi-based distribution platform also served safety guidance, folder-managed media and a large LED matrix display.
