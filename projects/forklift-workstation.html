Forklift-Mounted Workstation Power Integration

Replaced a short-runtime UPS arrangement with a direct, fused DC power system that allowed a forklift-mounted thermal label printer and Windows tablet computer to operate throughout the shift without UPS recharge cycles.

32 VDCDC-DC Converter24 V Printer20 V ComputerCurrent Control
Final integrated forklift workstationA mobile computing and label-printing station powered from the forklift electrical system rather than relying on swappable UPS units.

Original limitation

The UPS provided roughly two hours of runtime during an eight-hour shift, requiring repeated charging and spare units to keep the workstation operating.

My role

Power-system concept, component selection, DC conversion, fused distribution, switched integration, commissioning and current-limit troubleshooting.

Project overview

Three forklift workstations depended on portable UPS units to power a 24 V thermal label printer and a 20 V Windows tablet, creating repeated charging, runtime and cost problems. I redesigned the power approach around the forklift’s 32 V electrical system so both loads could operate reliably through the shift without rotating UPS units.

01 · Operating problem

A temporary backup device had become the permanent power strategy

Workstation loadsA 24 V / 3 A thermal label printer and a 20 V / 2.5 A tablet-style Windows computer were mounted on the forklift.
UPS runtimeEach UPS supplied about two hours of backup, far shorter than the eight-hour operating shift.
Operational workaroundTo avoid downtime, each workstation needed one UPS on the forklift and another available while charging.
Cost pressureEach UPS cost about $1,200. With three forklift setups and two UPS units per setup, the workaround was expensive as well as operationally awkward.

02 · Power-system redesign

Use the forklift as the energy source, then condition and protect the loads

32 V Forklift Battery→Key-Switched Supply→Manual Toggle→20 A DC-DC Conversion→Fused Loads
SourceThe forklift electrical system provided approximately 32 VDC.
ConversionA 20 A DC-DC converter was used as the core of the regulated power solution.
ProtectionSeparate fusing was added for the connected equipment, and the converter included adjustable current control.
Operator controlThe system was enabled with the forklift key and also included a separate on/off toggle switch.
Parts cost for the replacement power solution was approximately $110 per setup, compared with roughly $2,400 in UPS hardware required to keep one workstation continuously supported with one unit in service and one charging.

03 · Commissioning & troubleshooting

Field testing exposed the final adjustment

Initial resultThe computer and printer powered correctly from the forklift system, eliminating the need for UPS swapping and recharge cycles.
Observed issueThe thermal printer occasionally produced faded labels even though the workstation remained powered.
DiagnosisThe printer drew more current during active printing than during idle operation, so the converter's current setting was limiting peak demand.
CorrectionAfter the current setting was adjusted, print quality stabilized and the workstation operated correctly through the shift.

Engineering takeaway

Replace a recurring workaround with a system-level solution
